CORRELATION VS COVARIANCE IN STATISTICS
======================================

1. COVARIANCE
------------
Definition:
Covariance measures how two variables vary together.

Interpretation:
- Positive covariance: the variables tend to move in the same direction.
- Negative covariance: the variables tend to move in opposite directions.
- Covariance near zero: there is little or no linear co-variation; a nonlinear relationship may still exist.

Sample covariance formula:
             sum[(x_i - x_bar)(y_i - y_bar)]
 s_xy =      --------------------------------
                         n - 1

Population covariance formula:
             sum[(x_i - mu_x)(y_i - mu_y)]
 Cov(X,Y) =  ------------------------------
                           N

Here, x_bar and y_bar are sample means; mu_x and mu_y are population means.
Use n - 1 for sample covariance and N for population covariance.

Key properties:
- Its value has units equal to (units of X) x (units of Y).
- Its magnitude depends on the variables' scales and units.
- The sign indicates the direction of linear co-variation, but the magnitude is difficult to compare across different datasets.

2. CORRELATION
--------------
Definition:
Correlation measures the direction and strength of the linear relationship between two variables.

Pearson sample correlation formula:
                 s_xy
 r =      -------------------
          s_x * s_y

Equivalently, r is sample covariance divided by the product of the sample standard deviations.

Interpretation of Pearson's r:
- r = +1: perfect positive linear relationship.
- r = -1: perfect negative linear relationship.
- r = 0: no linear relationship (but a nonlinear relationship may still exist).
- Values closer to +1 or -1 indicate a stronger linear relationship; values closer to 0 indicate a weaker linear relationship.

Key properties:
- It is unitless (dimensionless).
- It always lies between -1 and +1, inclusive.
- It is unaffected by changes of measurement units that preserve the direction of the variables (for example, centimetres to metres).
- Pearson correlation describes linear association and can be sensitive to outliers.

3. MAIN DIFFERENCES
-------------------
Feature                 Covariance                  Correlation
Meaning                 How variables vary together Direction and strength of linear association
Range                   No fixed range              -1 to +1 for Pearson's r
Units                   Product of variable units   Unitless
Scale dependence        Magnitude depends on scale  Standardized; easier to compare
Interpretation          Sign is usually most useful Sign and magnitude are interpretable

4. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THEM
----------------------------
Correlation standardizes covariance:

                 Cov(X,Y)
 Corr(X,Y) = -------------------
             SD(X) * SD(Y)

For sample data, use sample covariance and sample standard deviations consistently.
If either variable has zero standard deviation (it is constant), correlation is undefined.

5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE
-----------------
Suppose study hours and exam scores tend to increase together.
- Their covariance would generally be positive.
- Their Pearson correlation would be positive, with a value closer to +1 if the relationship is strongly linear.

If temperature is measured in Celsius or Fahrenheit, covariance's numerical value changes because the scale changes. Pearson correlation remains the same under a positive linear change of units.

6. WHEN TO USE EACH
-------------------
Use covariance when:
- You need to know whether two variables tend to vary in the same or opposite directions.
- You are working with variance-covariance matrices or statistical models that require covariance values.

Use correlation when:
- You want an easy-to-interpret, standardized measure of linear association.
- You want to compare the strength of linear associations measured on different scales.

7. IMPORTANT CAUTIONS
--------------------
- Correlation does not prove causation.
- A correlation of zero does not rule out a nonlinear relationship.
- Outliers can strongly affect both covariance and Pearson correlation.
- Association does not mean every observation follows the overall trend.

QUICK MEMORY TIP
----------------
Covariance = co-movement, with a scale-dependent magnitude.
Correlation = standardized covariance, with a value from -1 to +1.
